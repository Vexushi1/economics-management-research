# Platform competition and Hotelling differentiation — reference

**Scope:** platform/retailer distribution structures, consumer heterogeneity and product differentiation with potentially endogenous platform participation. Classical spatial differentiation is a *model family*, not a universal demand formula.

Before drafting a model:

- Declare each platform, seller, and consumer type; location or taste heterogeneity; outside option; and whether consumers single-home or multi-home.
- Specify consumer utilities and any network externality in explicitly bounded AST terms, and whether fees/retail prices are controlled by platform or seller.
- Check when retail prices and participation are observed; never treat equilibrium actions of the same simultaneous stage as ex ante observable without an explicit information model.
- Check marginal consumer position, coverage, market-share bounds, multiple adoption equilibria and corners separately. Neither full-market coverage nor unique equilibrium is assumed.
- Document source of each utility function, dimension units, demand feasibility and comparison with no-platform or no-network-effect special cases.

**Baseline sources:** H. Hotelling, “Stability in Competition,” *Economic Journal* (1929); [pAI-Econ-codex](https://github.com/Cavaradossi/pAI-Econ-codex/tree/84385fb1b4e093b22acae7fd7a8a5381762fb638) as a workflow reference. No code or formula has been copied from another Skill.

**Phase 3 limit:** Hidden participation types, discontinuous best responses and fixed-point existence need additional economic semantics and Phase 4 proofs; a valid JSON tree alone does not establish those properties.
